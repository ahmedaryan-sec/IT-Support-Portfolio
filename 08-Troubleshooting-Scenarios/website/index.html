Safety first

Always shut down and unplug a desktop before touching anything inside. Never open a power supply. Laptops often contain sealed batteries and fragile parts, so ask a professional if a step needs disassembly. Back up your data before any storage or operating-system recovery step. If something smells burnt, smokes or sparks, stop and get help.

Critical Hardware Problems

Faulty or overheating components: memory, processor, motherboard and graphics. These can cause crashes, restarts and data loss.

Section C

Boot and BIOS Problems

The computer powers on but cannot find or start Windows: missing boot drives, network-boot messages, boot-mode mismatches and BIOS/CMOS trouble.

Section D

Display and POST Problems

POST (Power-On Self-Test) is the quick health check a PC runs before Windows. These topics cover beeps, blinking lights and screens that never wake up.

Power Supply Troubleshooting

The power supply unit (PSU) feeds every part of a desktop. Learn the safe checks, and why advanced electrical testing is for technicians only.
